# Stacy × Agent MAXX — BluSky GrillMe Interview

Purpose: turn Stacy's real relationships, observations, history, and instincts into verified business opportunities without making him write a prompt or fill out a technical form.

This follows the Matt Pocock GrillMe/grilling pattern:
- inspect known context before asking;
- ask **one question at a time**;
- walk unresolved decision branches instead of dumping a questionnaire;
- include MAXX's current recommended interpretation when useful;
- do not ask Stacy for something the repo/public evidence can answer;
- save durable answers as the conversation resolves them;
- stop when the next business test is clear enough to run.

## Trigger

Use this interview automatically when Stacy mentions BluSky/Blue Sky/Blusky and any of these are true:
- he says he knows someone there;
- he remembers how they used to work;
- he knows customers, partners, property managers, vendors, insurance people, or employees around them;
- he says “I think they need…” or describes a problem without evidence;
- an opportunity depends on relationship strength or internal reality;
- the next move is unclear because MAXX lacks human context.

Do not announce “I am starting GrillMe.” Ask naturally.

## Interview stance

MAXX is not conducting a survey. It is collaborating with Stacy like a business partner.

Good:
> “Who’s the person at BluSky you know best, and what was your actual relationship with them?”

Then listen, summarize the useful fact, and ask the next best question.

Bad:
> “Please answer these 18 intake questions.”

## Phase 1 — Relationship map

Start here unless already known.

Candidate questions, one at a time:

1. **Who at BluSky do you know best?**
   - Follow-up: What is/was their role?
   - Follow-up: How did you meet/work together?
   - Follow-up: How recently have you talked?
   - Follow-up: Would they take your call today?

2. **Who else do you know around that person?**
   Explore:
   - office leadership;
   - business development;
   - project managers;
   - estimators;
   - operations;
   - finance/AR;
   - property managers;
   - insurance people;
   - subcontractors/trade partners;
   - vendors;
   - former employees.

3. **What have you personally seen about how BluSky makes money?**
   Separate direct observation from assumptions.

4. **What kinds of jobs or customers did your contact care about most?**

5. **What did they complain about, struggle with, or spend too much time doing?**

Record facts in `SESSION_NOTES.md`; candidate opportunities go to `OPPORTUNITY_LEDGER.md`.

## Phase 2 — Money map

Once relationship context is clear, follow the money.

Ask one branch at a time:
- Where do their best jobs come from?
- Is the real buyer a homeowner, property manager, insurer, facility manager, portfolio owner, broker, or someone else?
- What makes one relationship valuable repeatedly rather than once?
- Do they make more money from emergency mitigation, reconstruction, roofing, environmental work, renovation, large-loss work, or something else?
- Where does a job stall before becoming revenue?
- Where does earned revenue stall before becoming cash?
- What customer type is expensive to acquire?
- Which existing relationship could represent multiple buildings or locations?

MAXX should propose a working interpretation when helpful:
> “It sounds like the property manager may be more valuable than the individual loss because they control multiple buildings. Is that how you saw it?”

## Phase 3 — Seven leak scan

Use this only after Stacy has given concrete context.

Ask whether any of these happen, without assuming they do:
1. missed or slow inbound response;
2. old leads/accounts never followed up;
3. estimates/proposals with no next action;
4. customers using only one property/service/region;
5. trade/vendor capacity discovered too late;
6. claim/AR/documentation follow-up dragging cash collection;
7. event/association relationships captured but not systematically converted.

For each “yes,” ask for one real example.

## Phase 4 — Black Swan interview

The goal is to find leverage that public research cannot reveal.

### Concealed known
> “What problem do you think the people inside BluSky already know about but probably wouldn’t put on the website?”

### Hidden constraint
> “When they know what they should do but it still doesn’t happen, what usually gets in the way?”

Possible branches: staffing, after-hours coverage, approvals, claims, documentation, estimator capacity, corporate rules, CRM discipline, trade capacity, budget, relationship ownership.

### Hidden agenda
> “If your contact could make one number look much better this quarter, what number would actually matter to them?”

### Positive leverage
> “What would make your contact look like a hero inside the company?”

### Negative leverage
> “What recurring problem would they pay to stop dealing with?”

### Network leverage
> “Who do you know that can open five doors instead of one?”

### Owned-data leverage
> “What information do they already have that nobody is really working?”

### Cost leverage
> “Where could we save them more money than we could make them with another marketing campaign?”

## Phase 5 — Opportunity sharpening

When a candidate emerges, MAXX asks enough to fill:

- **WHO:** exact person/team/account;
- **PROBLEM:** observed or reported problem;
- **ECONOMIC EVENT:** meeting, job, account expansion, cash collection, reduced sourcing time, etc.;
- **EVIDENCE:** Stacy observation, public source, internal data, or unknown;
- **RELATIONSHIP:** how Stacy can credibly enter the conversation;
- **ASK:** smallest useful first request;
- **TEST:** what can be tried in 7–14 days;
- **PROOF:** what result would count;
- **RISK:** what could damage trust;
- **OWNER:** who must approve it.

If these cannot be answered, keep the opportunity in `research_more` rather than pretending it is ready.

## Phase 6 — First-conversation design

Prefer a useful first conversation over a sales pitch.

Examples:
- “Can I show you a quick idea around old relationships you already have?”
- “What part of the Seattle pipeline is hardest to keep on top of?”
- “Would it be useful if we took 50 old opportunities and showed you the 10 that deserve a human follow-up?”
- “If we found one place money is stuck, would you let us prove it on a small batch?”

Never claim MAXX has found a leak until evidence supports it.

## Interview stop rule

Stop questioning when MAXX can state:

> “Here is the person, here is the relationship, here is the problem hypothesis, here is the evidence we still need, and here is the smallest test worth asking permission to run.”

Then summarize in plain language and ask Stacy to approve/correct the proposed next move.

## Persistence rule

After each meaningful answer:
- durable person/relationship facts -> `SESSION_NOTES.md`;
- opportunity hypothesis -> `OPPORTUNITY_LEDGER.md`;
- strategy-changing discovery -> `STRATEGY.md`;
- do not overwrite prior truth silently; mark corrections and dates.

## Onboarding reuse

When Agent MAXX onboarding is later built for Stacy, reuse this pattern for other relationship domains:

`PERSON -> HISTORY -> WHAT STACY KNOWS -> MONEY FLOW -> FRICTION -> BLACK SWAN -> SMALLEST TEST -> PROOF`

BluSky is the first domain-specific implementation, not a one-off questionnaire.
