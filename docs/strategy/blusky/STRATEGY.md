## Strategic thesis

Do not begin with “How do we get BluSky more random leads?”

Start with:

> **Where is BluSky Seattle already close to money, but losing time, follow-up, account penetration, capacity, or cash?**

This produces a stronger set of opportunities because restoration economics are often relationship-, portfolio-, response-, insurance-, vendor-, and operationally driven.

## Opportunity lanes

These lanes are hypotheses. Stacy's lived knowledge and authorized BluSky data decide which one becomes the actual pilot.

### 1. Owned Relationship Reactivation

Possible inputs:
- dormant CRM contacts;
- previous business-development relationships;
- old proposals/estimates;
- association/event contacts BluSky is authorized to use;
- customers not contacted recently;
- partners Stacy personally knows.

Goal:
**recover a qualified meeting or reopened account from relationships BluSky already owns.**

Why this can beat cold lead generation:
- lower acquisition cost;
- existing trust/context;
- easier to verify outcome;
- Stacy's relationship knowledge becomes useful immediately.

### 2. Portfolio Preparedness Pipeline

Target categories:
- multifamily owners/property managers;
- commercial property/facility managers;
- senior living / healthcare facilities;
- hospitality portfolios;
- insurance relationships where policy allows.

Offer concept:
**15-Minute Property Disaster Readiness Review**

Conversation is before the disaster:
`preparedness -> property/portfolio context -> preferred response relationship -> future project opportunity`

Do not prospect victims during an active disaster.

### 3. Existing Account Expansion

Ask:
- Which customers use BluSky at only one property?
- Which accounts use only one BluSky service?
- Which customer organizations control properties in other BluSky markets?
- Which current account has a corporate or regional relationship above the site level?

Goal:
**turn one relationship into additional properties, services, regions, or enterprise relationships.**

### 4. Inbound Recovery

Potential leakage:
- missed calls;
- forms not followed promptly;
- after-hours non-emergency inquiries;
- estimate/proposal follow-up;
- business-development-to-operations handoffs;
- unanswered emails;
- inquiries with no explicit next action.

Goal:
**every qualified inquiry receives a verified next action without interfering with emergency response.**

### 5. Trade Partner Capacity Scout

Potential use:
- maintain an evidence-backed bench of plumbers, electricians, painters, framers, roofers, remediation specialists, labor providers, and other trades;
- prioritize by geography, service, licensing/certification evidence, contact path, and readiness;
- reduce emergency sourcing time during surge events.

Agent MAXX does **not** replace BluSky's credentialing, insurance, safety, quality, or vendor-approval process.

### 6. Aged AR / Claim Follow-Up Intelligence

With finance authorization only:
`aged AR -> normalize -> identify blocker -> rank recoverable value -> human next action -> outcome`

Possible blockers to discover:
- missing documentation;
- claim status;
- approval bottleneck;
- wrong contact;
- internal handoff;
- unresolved scope/change order;
- payer/insurance follow-up.

No autonomous debt collection, legal threats, or insurer representation.

### 7. M&A / Restoration Whitespace Scout

BluSky has a public history of growth through mergers/acquisitions.

Only if corporate development expresses interest:
- identify geographic/service whitespace;
- research independent restoration operators;
- produce evidence packets;
- do not contact owners until BluSky authorizes the motion.

## First offer to BluSky

### BluSky Seattle Revenue Recovery Pilot

Plain-language promise:

> Give us one small slice of relationships, inquiries, accounts, or receivables you already have. We will show you where money may be getting lost or left untouched and return a ranked human-action queue. We prove one recovery path in Seattle before asking you to scale anything.

Do not sell “AI lead generation” as the primary value.

## Pilot input hierarchy

Ask for the smallest authorized dataset that can prove value:

1. dormant CRM / old business-development contacts;
2. old estimates or proposals;
3. recent event contacts BluSky is authorized to use;
4. missed/unresolved inbound inquiries;
5. selected existing accounts for cross-sell/portfolio mapping;
6. small aged-AR export with finance approval;
7. trade-partner sourcing problem with operations approval.

Never ask for the full CRM first.

## Proof gate

The pilot is valuable only if one real owner/operator can verify at least one:
- meeting booked;
- portfolio/preparedness review accepted;
- dormant account reopened;
- existing account expanded;
- missed inquiry recovered;
- qualified partner added with less sourcing work;
- receivable/claim advanced or cash recovered;
- clear disqualifying reason that prevents wasted effort.

Track baseline, time, cost, actions, outcome, false positives, and repeatability.

## Black Swan questions for every lane

1. What does the operator already know is broken?
2. What are they embarrassed/tired of dealing with repeatedly?
3. What does corporate measure that local teams actually feel?
4. Where is money already earned but delayed?
5. Which relationship controls many properties/accounts?
6. What task becomes painful only during storms or catastrophe surges?
7. Which customer uses only 10–20% of what BluSky could provide?
8. Where does information die between sales, operations, estimators, claims, finance, and trades?
9. What would save more money than a marketing campaign could make?
10. What can Stacy tell us that no public dataset can?

## Stop conditions

Do not add software because an idea sounds useful.
Do not launch national outreach before Seattle proof.
Do not call a public business record a qualified opportunity.
Do not automate external communication without approval.
Do not confuse relationship discovery with permission to contact.
