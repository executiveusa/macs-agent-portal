# Agent MAXX 006 — Stacy Field Guide

> **Talk normally. MAXX handles the machines underneath.**

This is the non-technical onboarding source for Stacy. It follows the live MAXX product contract rather than exposing implementation details.

## 60-second start

Open MAXX and say:

**“Max, tell me what I should handle first today.”**

You do not need to learn models, prompts, tools, or agent architecture. Start with the outcome you want.

## Four starter requests

1. **30-day campaign** — “Build me a 30-day Instagram campaign and interview me for what you need first.”
2. **Website rescue** — “Get this website live safely and show me proof that it works.”
3. **Video flywheel** — “Turn this video into a week of useful content for my business.”
4. **Follow-up recovery** — “Find the leads and follow-ups we are missing, then recommend what to do first.”

## A strong MAXX request

When it matters, give MAXX four things:

- **Outcome** — what should be true at the end?
- **Context** — who or what is this about?
- **Boundary** — what must he not touch, publish, or spend?
- **Proof** — how will you know it worked?

Example:

> “Max, get the client website live. Do not change email or DNS unless you prove it is necessary. Test mobile and the contact form when you are done.”

## Pups

Pups are persistent specialist teammates **inside MAXX**. Use one when a role or job keeps coming back. Use a normal MAXX request for one-off work.

### Chief Pup
Keeps the active work small and coordinated. It delegates only when specialization actually helps.

### Superdoer Pup
Prepares useful work now: drafts, meeting prep, research, plans, assets, and internal deliverables.

### Business-in-a-Box Pup
Works one owner-supplied business idea in this order: offer → customer → distribution → payment → delivery → retention → automation.

### What “always on” means
A Pup can keep working on the server while Stacy’s phone or computer is closed. It does **not** mean a Pup can publish, buy, delete, change permissions, or expose secrets without the normal MAXX approval boundary.

Try:

> “Max, spin up a Pup to keep our social content moving every week. Prepare the drafts, but nothing publishes without me.”

## MAXX Mode

Use MAXX Mode for decisions that deserve more reasoning: strategy, difficult comparisons, architecture, or high-stakes tradeoffs.

MAXX Mode gives the problem more attention; it does **not** remove safety or approvals.

Try:

> “Max, activate MAXX Mode. Give me 3 options, the 2 tradeoffs that actually matter, and your 1 recommendation.”

## Second Brain

Use **Settings → Your second brain** to upload useful history such as AI exports, notes, documents, CSVs, or ZIP archives. MAXX organizes large uploads privately and later loads only the pieces relevant to the current conversation.

The goal is simple: **stop re-explaining your business.**

Try:

> “Max, check my second brain and remind me what we already decided about this opportunity.”

## What MAXX can help run

- **Follow-up recovery** — missed calls, forms, donors, volunteers, leads, and stale conversations.
- **Social campaigns** — interview first, plan, draft, prepare media, then publish/schedule only through the approval boundary.
- **Video production** — clips, captions, summaries, and useful content from longer media.
- **Website operations** — audit, repair, deploy, verify, and preserve rollback.
- **Research and decisions** — compare options, challenge assumptions, and recommend the strongest path.
- **Repeatable routines** — teach a Pup once and let it prepare the same kind of work on a schedule.

## Approval rule

**GO:** research, organize, compare, draft, test, prepare proof.

**ASK:** publish, send, spend money, delete, or change important settings.

**STOP:** target unclear, credential missing, or the action could cause material damage.

Try:

> “Max, prepare everything. If anything goes public or costs money, stop and ask me first.”

## MAXX should not just agree

For meaningful decisions, MAXX can use **3-2-1**:

- 3 viable options;
- 2 tradeoffs that actually change the decision;
- 1 recommendation — the path most likely to work, not the answer most likely to please you.

Try:

> “Max, don’t tell me what I want to hear. Give me the strongest recommendation and tell me what evidence would change your mind.”

## Phone, voice, and MAXX Eyes

MAXX is designed as a mobile-first conversation. Stacy can talk or type, and the private app is install-shaped for Android.

**MAXX Eyes** is the coming-soon vision input: phone camera and compatible glasses can show MAXX what Stacy is looking at. Vision is another input to the same MAXX — not a separate brain.

## Four status words

- **Working** — the job is moving.
- **Needs you** — a real approval, decision, or connection is blocking the next move.
- **Done** — the outcome is complete; open proof for details.
- **Blocked** — MAXX could not safely finish and should explain the next fix.

## Stacy’s first week

1. Ask MAXX to rank the top 3 things worth handling this week.
2. Give him one real task and require proof.
3. Upload important history into Second Brain.
4. Create **one** Pup for a repeating job.
5. Use MAXX Mode on one important decision.

## The whole cheat sheet

**JUST TALK.** Say the result you want.

**USE MAXX MODE.** When the problem is genuinely hard.

**MAKE A PUP.** When a useful role should keep coming back.

**UPLOAD HISTORY.** So MAXX can stop asking you to repeat the business.

**LOOK FOR PROOF.** “Done” should mean something actually happened.

### First move

> **“Max, give me one win today that saves time, recovers money, or prevents something from slipping through the cracks.”**
